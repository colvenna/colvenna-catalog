What the failures are (section 5 has the numbers; they change no verdict):

- **P1a and P1b, the commit's cost at the client.** Of a whole commit's 798 ms (p50, EKS), the three REST requests it makes (two snapshot loads and the commit) take 116 ms to their answers' headers (the sum of their medians); the rest, about 680 ms, is the client's own: its S3 writes of the commit's manifests and reading the answers. The serverless form shows the same, 861 ms of which 251 ms are the requests. Eight writers back to back therefore commit about 8.7 times a second on the EKS form whatever the catalog does, and the serverless form, at 8.07, passes its own target of 5. On one table (P1b) each of the four writers takes about 1.9 s a commit (p50, conflict retries included) against the 2 s the load offers, falls behind its schedule (a commit started up to 5 minutes late) and, after 10 refused attempts on a stale base, fails some (Paimon's `commit.max-retries`, 10): the catalog refused each stale base as it must, and no outcome was unknown. The terms' basis assumed 15 to 25 commits a second (terms.md, P1 "Why"); the measured client cost is what that assumption lacked.
- **P2 on the EKS form, Nagle's algorithm.** The client's request line, which ends at the answer's headers, gives `getTable` p50 5 ms and p95 9 ms at 200/s; the operation, which reads the answer, gives 54.5 and 64.8 ms. The 40 to 50 ms between them is the server's HTTP server holding the body back on a kept-alive connection (Nagle; [#37](https://github.com/colvenna/colvenna-catalog/issues/37)), fixed after the tag by [#41](https://github.com/colvenna/colvenna-catalog/pull/41) for the next candidate. The serverless form's reads, answered through API Gateway, show no such gap (section 5.3).

Whether the targets of P1a and P1b, and P5's credential misses, change for the next candidate is the requester's decision: the terms are confirmed and this document does not change them. They are put to the requester in [#1](https://github.com/colvenna/colvenna-catalog/issues/1).

## 4. Scenarios, method and targets

As [terms.md](terms.md) section 4 fixes them; the harness ([`tests/performance/README.md`](../../tests/performance/README.md)) runs each as one command, `perf.py run <scenario>`. In brief:

- **Client-observed latency** of a request is the Paimon client's own log line, per attempt; **operation latency** is the load tool's timing around one Paimon call, the client's retries included. Open-loop requests are timed from when each was due; a request is sent on time when it started within 100 ms of that.
- **Joins**: by the server's `X-Request-Id`, which the client's line carries; an operation's attempts are its thread's lines between its start and end. A credential request is a miss or a hit as its `ISSUE_DATA_TOKEN` audit event says; a commit refused on a stale base is a conflict, not an error.
- **Percentiles**: nearest rank over every sample of the window, warm-up excluded; 1,000 samples for a judged percentile (P4: 40).
- **Open-loop percentiles** are of the requests answered; those that failed are errors, counted apart. The client's request line ends at the answer's headers, so for P2 and P3 it is reported beside the judged latency, which includes reading the answer.
- **Choices the terms leave to the method**: P1b's whole commit is the `commit` call (conflict retries included); P3b's 20 tables are asked once each, then a minute of warm-up at the rate, before its window; P5's warm-up is 2 minutes; P5 reports apart the requests a serverless environment that started in the window served first.
- **What the terms cannot give as written**: P5's credential misses (10 % of 2 a second for 30 minutes, about 360) are fewer than the 1,000 a judged percentile needs, so that row is `NOT_JUDGED` and the candidate's verdict cannot be `PASS` unless the terms change for it (the requester's decision). Nothing was decided for this candidate: the terms ran unchanged, and the question is in [#1](https://github.com/colvenna/colvenna-catalog/issues/1).

No judged run differs from the terms' durations or loads: every `result.json` has `reduced: false` and `official: true`.

## 7. Limitations

As terms.md section 7, and of this measurement:

- One Region (`us-west-2`), the default sizes, synthetic load from inside the test environment's VPC; clients in other VPCs or Regions, bigger nodes, more replicas, a bigger database or more Lambda concurrency are not measured.
- The metadata database is a burstable `db.t4g.small`: each run's lowest credit balance is in section 5.7; no run spent its credits.
- STS, Cognito, API Gateway and Lambda account quotas are not measured.
- Lambda's idle reaping is not the test's to control: the idle requests of P4 are observed only. The judged cold starts are forced by new versions, as a deploy makes them.
- Engines' own work is not measured: a commit's latency here is the catalog's and the client's.
- The client's request line times an attempt from sending it to receiving the answer's headers; an operation's time includes reading the answer, the client's retries and, for open-loop requests, any wait after the time it was due. They differ much on the EKS form's reads (about 50 ms, the Nagle delay of section 5.3) and in the serverless form's P2 steps above its concurrency (the client's backoff on 429 and 503).
- P1a's and P1b's commit throughput is bounded by the client's own work per commit (section 5.1), which the harness does not split further than the requests' lines; how much of it is S3 and how much reading the answers is not measured.
- The installs were made for the acceptance on its own test environment, at once with the UAT on others (terms.md section 2 allows it); nothing else ran on them until their last run had ended; then the database secrets' rotations of the UAT report's section 8 ran on them, and they were torn down.
